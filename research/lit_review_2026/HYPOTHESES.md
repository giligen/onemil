## 1. What the 2022–2026 literature agrees on, for a small US-equity shop

Short-horizon predictability is real everywhere and dies inside the spread almost everywhere: 5-second OOS R² of 10.5% on S&P 100 names is gone in five minutes and 80% of it needs the last 10 ms (E-E4); 1-minute OFI forecasts have OOS R² ≈ −0.1% and 0.2–0.4%/yr gross P&L (E-E2); deep-LOB models forecast ~50 book events and collapse when the trend threshold is set to the spread (E-E9–E12); anything we would hold 1–5 minutes in a 20–100 bps-spread name is spread-bounded by construction (E §0). What survives costs is (i) index-level, once-a-day, deep-instrument timing — the Zarattini noise-area rule on SPY at a realistic net Sharpe of 0.6–0.9 (A-R20 SR 0.91 ES with Databento fills, A-R21 SR 0.40 SPY at 1×, A-R22 the authors' own 2026 SPY base SR 0.87, vs 1.33 published at 4× with $0.001/share slippage) and the 15-minute factor-zoo SPY forecaster at net SR 1.37 (E-E15, infrastructure we lack); (ii) liquidity-provision effects whose gross premium is payment for immediacy and therefore persists but is VIX-dependent and has trended down with spreads: cross-sectional end-of-day reversal, entirely from the loser/long side, 3.78 bps/day VW, 14.7 bps/day in the smallest size quintile, every 3-year window since 1993 (B-1.10/C-C5); the volume-shock overnight premium, ~14%/yr SR 0.89 net of IB costs in a practitioner Russell-3000 replication (D-V2); short-term residual reversal (D-R1–R3); (iii) the cross-sectional overnight-vs-intraday tug of war, huge gross (+3.47%/mo overnight α, t 16.8, C-B1) but "insufficient to overcome 1 bp per trade" since 2014 (C-A4) — an order-timing rule, not a book. What does not survive: index overnight drift (≈ 0 since 2021, imbalance dispersion −55%, C-A5); unconditional market intraday momentum in the 0DTE era (1,085 SPX sessions 2022–26, slope +0.006, t 0.6, flat every year, A-R17/C-C3; long-gamma dealers produce order-flow *reversal*, A-R13; gamma's vol impact is +3.3 pp at most, A-R14/E-E22); single-stock intraday momentum in retail-dominated names (institutional flow drives it, B-1.14; the last half hour reverses, B-1.10); gap-and-go in single stocks (attention gap-ups open high and fade, B-1.8/C-B4; overnight jumps negatively predict the next day, t ≈ −11, B-1.16 — index gaps continue, single-stock attention gaps do not, B-1.17); raw ORB/breakouts on index futures (no variant passes walk-forward, A-R18) and on the whole stock market (R4 base SR 0.48; the edge is *selection* by relative volume, +0.08R at RV ≥ 1, +0.38R at RV > 30× on 20–40 bps stops in 1–3 bps-spread names — a loss of ~1R/trade at our 40 bps, B §3); LETF end-of-day flow (net SR 0.05, decaying, A-R11); retail-flow following (mis-signed 28%, dead 2016–21, −14.8%/yr inside retail-heavy names, E-E29–E31); VPIN (a BVC artefact, E-E34); PEAD and the earnings-announcement premium ex-microcaps (D-E1/E2); turn-of-month (D-S1). Decay calibration: −26% out-of-sample and −58% post-publication for US cross-sectional predictors (D-M1); the average anomaly nets 4–8 bps/month, ≈ 50% gross decay, 72% ex-stale years, 93% net of costs (D-M2); D's working rule is 0.4× the published gross, test only if the haircut gross is ≥ 3× our round-trip cost, and treat any result above the published number as a look-ahead bug (D §1, §10). The base rate for any new discretionary intraday rule: 97% of persistent retail day traders lose; the skilled minority nets ~28 bps/day at Sharpe 0.2 (B-1.4/1.5).

## 3. Priority order for testing (prior × testability × capacity)

Rationale weights: prior = the realistic column, not the paper column; testability = which store already holds the data and how many events it gives on 2025–26; capacity = whether a 4-slot, $60K, 2×-margin, 1–3 s account can hold the trade at the size that makes the prior worth anything.

1. **M6 (A-H3) noise-area SPY, unlevered, VWAP + band stop.** The only rule in all five reviews with an independently replicated net Sharpe ≥ 0.6 (A-R20/R21/R22) and an author-reported post-publication year (A-R1 FAQ Q24). ETF1m gives 2016–24 estimation + 2025–26 holdout, ≈ 450 trades/yr, public code for parity. Capacity unlimited (SPY). Fills at next-minute open, 1 bp/trade, or the number is fiction (A-H3).
2. **M1/M2/M3/M5/M12 (A-H1/H2/H2b/H7/H11, C-H-C5/C15, D-D4) — the last-30-min and overnight-SPY family in one script on ETF1m.** Prior ≈ 0 unconditional (A-R17), but it is a one-evening regression that decides whether the conditional forms (M4, M5) deserve an OI pull, and a clean 2016–26 negative on the window our own HOD/ORB exits sit in is worth having (A §4.2). Capacity unlimited; dollars tiny (§4).
3. **M22 (D-D1, B-H-B7) volume-shock overnight.** Strongest daily-bar prior (D-V2 net SR 0.89 on R3000), long-only, auction fills, DAILY has every input, hundreds of event-days for power. Capacity is the caveat: the published Sharpe is a broad-book number; a 4-slot version is dominated by idiosyncratic overnight variance (§4). Report the breadth-vs-slots trade-off explicitly.
4. **M16 (C-H-C8/C9) the single-stock gap table.** A measurement no paper provides, DAILY + NEWS, an afternoon, and it feeds directly into how ORB/HOD treat gap-ups (C §6). Validate `open` against auction prints first (C header, C §5.1).
5. **M18 (B-H-B10, C-H-C3 no-chase) delayed entry on prior-day attention names.** Free, a rule about our existing books, RANGE1m has the paths; three independent sources for the mechanism (C-A1, C-B4, B-1.10 T11).
6. **M20 (B-H-B5, C-H-C4, D-D5) end-of-day loser reversal.** RANGE1m is exactly the extreme-ROD3 sample with no selection bias for a loser-side rule (B §5); the effect is the best-documented cross-sectional intraday effect (C-C5, t 27 in the smallest quintile); but 40 bps rt vs 10–20 bps gross means only the untested ≤ −8% tail can pay (B-H-B5). 7. **M9 (B-H-B2) RelVol monotonicity in our universe.** Cheapest sanity check that decides whether any ORB-family work on RANGE1m is worth continuing (B §4 ranking); needs a 14-day OR-volume fetch.

## 4. What would move the needle to 10R/week

Units. 1R = $100 of risk per trade, so 10R/week = $1,000/week ≈ $50K/yr. Account $60K with 2× intraday margin → $120K maximum notional; overnight sleeves also get Reg-T 2× but sit against maintenance. For full-notional ETF sleeves "R = $100" is not the natural unit: a 30-min SPY hold at $120K has a per-trade sd of ≈ 30–40 bps ≈ $360–480 (4–5R of sd per trade), and the noise-area rule at 2× has a daily sd of ≈ 1% of equity ≈ $580 (A-R1 vol 7.7%/yr unlevered). Everything below is the realistic-prior column of §2 at full deployment; nothing is a forecast.

**The scale of published intraday effects in dollars.** "2–15 bps/day on a liquid instrument" (A §2.1, C §0, D §10) on the whole $120K book, every day: 2 bps = $24/day = $120/week = **1.2R/week**; 15 bps = $180/day = $900/week = **9R/week**. Reaching 10R/week from index-timing alone therefore needs ≥ 17 bps/day net on the full 2× book every session — a level no source reports net after 2022 on any single instrument (the largest is A-R1's 12 bps/day at 1×, before the replication haircut; E-E15's 19.9%/yr intraday ≈ 8 bps/day net is infrastructure-gated).

**Honest sum.** The intraday SPY sleeves (M6 + M2/M4) and the overnight sleeves (M22 or M29, M36) can stack in time (flat at 16:00, MOC in), so the expectations add: conservative ends ≈ 1 + 0.3 + 3 + 0 + 0 ≈ **4R/week**, optimistic ends of the *realistic* priors ≈ 2 + 0.9 + 8 + 2.4 + 5 ≈ **18R/week** — but with a combined weekly sd of ≈ 70–80R dominated by the 4-name overnight book. At 10R/week mean and 75R/week sd the weekly Sharpe is 0.13 (≈ 0.95 annualised), and distinguishing that mean from zero at 2σ takes (2 × 75 / 10)² ≈ 225 weeks. Three things follow. (1) Index timing alone tops out at ≈ 1–3R/week at 2× on $60K; it is where the priors are best and the dollars are smallest. (2) The two sleeves that can reach 10R/week in expectation are M22 (only at a breadth we cannot hold in 4 slots) and M20 (only if the untested ≤ −8% tail clears 40 bps — the published magnitudes say it does not). (3) None of the reviewed literature, at retail cost, beats the project's own HOD-break spec-sim (+0.27–0.33R at 23–27 trades/week on VAL/TEST per D-B1 citing `research/bf_zero/REPORT.md` — a relative tool, not a forecast); the review's realistic contribution to that book is cost reduction (M18, M44, M50) and vetoes (M25, M28, M33), not a second alpha source. For 10R/week to be more than noise on this account the source that would have to be true is B-H-B5's tail extrapolation, and it must be measured before it is believed.
