# Topic A — Intraday momentum and opening-range breakout on index ETFs / leveraged ETFs (2022–2026, plus foundations)

Literature review written 2026-09-16 for the OneMil research program. Sources were read from primary PDFs where obtainable (Gao et al. 2017 WP version of the JFE paper; Baltussen et al. JFE 2021; Li–Sakkas–Urquhart WP version; Barbon et al. SSRN; Zarattini "Beat the Market" v. 3 Feb 2025; Zarattini "Profitable Day Trading" 16 Feb 2024; Dim–Eraker–Vilkov May 2024; Park–Zhao Sept 2025; Aleti–Bollerslev–Siggaard Mgmt Sci 2025; Amaya et al. Jan 2025; Baltussen–Da–Soebhag Apr 2025; Limkriangkrai et al. PBFJ 2023). SSRN abstract pages were blocked (403), so for **Zarattini–Aziz 2023 (ORB, VWAP)**, **Maróy 2025** and **Rosa 2022** the rule/number extraction relies on the authors' own site, Crossref metadata, and independent secondary write-ups (CXO Advisory, danfin.net, QuantConnect, Quantitativo). Those rows are marked `[secondary]` and should be re-verified against the PDF before any number is used in a decision.

Conventions: "gross" = before any trading cost; "IS" = in-sample; "OOS" = out-of-sample; "bps/day" = mean daily strategy return in basis points; N = number of trades or daily observations. Where I judge a result as data-mined, gross-only, or under-powered I say so in the last column.

## 2. Cross-paper reading (what the evidence actually says)

1. **The index-level last-half-hour effect is small (≈2.5–3 bps/day gross) and regime-dependent.** Gao (1993–2013): 6.67%/yr gross on a 30-min window = 2.6 bps/day; Baltussen: ≈2.7 bps/day for equity futures; Baltussen–Da–Soebhag (to ~2023): TS component +2.8 bps/day. Net of a 1 bp SPY round-trip that leaves ≈1.5–2 bps/day. Rosa (2022) finds the overnight→last-half-hour version dead OOS after 2013 except in high-vol regimes; FirmTape (2022–26) finds the open→15:30 slope flat every year; Adams et al. (2025) give a mechanism for *anti*-momentum in the 0DTE era (long-gamma dealers → order-flow reversal). **Prior for an unconditional H1/H2 on 2025–26 data: near zero.**

2. **What survives is conditional**: high VIX / high first-half-hour vol (Gao R² 3.3%, Zarattini SR 3.5 at VIX>40, Rosa's high-vol state, Li et al.), macro-news days (Gao R² 11% on FOMC days), short-gamma days (Baltussen; FirmTape slope 0.055 t 3.1 on 15% of days; Park–Zhao), narrow-range setups (NR4 22 bps t 5.1). Every conditioning variable listed was found in-sample; only the VIX one appears in more than two independent papers.

3. **The Zarattini SPY noise-area model is the only intraday rule with (author-reported) post-publication numbers** (+17% May–Dec 2024 in the same simulator) and two independent cost-inclusive replications (SR 0.9 on ES with Databento fills; 0.4 on SPY with QC fills at 1×). The authors' own 2026 SPY baseline is SR 0.87. **Realistic prior: SR 0.6–0.9 unlevered-equivalent, hit ≈37–43%, ≈1.8 trades/day, ≈2–4 bps/trade gross.** The 1.33 needs 4× leverage on a vol-target and $0.001/share slippage.

4. **ORB on index ETFs/futures**: the only academic support is Tsai et al. 2019 (no stops, no costs) and the Zarattini QQQ/TQQQ paper (zero slippage, 24% win rate, tight stop). The one walk-forward, cost-inclusive test (Mesfin 2026, MNQ 2021–25) rejects every ORB variant. R4's own base case (all stocks, no selection) is SR 0.48 — the edge is stock *selection* (relative volume), not the breakout, which matches our own ORB veto-study finding.

5. **LETF end-of-day flow**: real, but Barbon et al. show the LETF-flow trading strategy nets Sharpe 0.05 after 25%-of-quoted-spread costs and its significance decays through 2012–19; gamma-based signals are what persist. Do not build an "LETF rebalancing momentum" rule on index ETFs.

6. **Trade-count/power**: Gao's daily rule gives one observation per day. Our local SPY store (2025-01-02 → 2026-03-20) is ≈305 sessions. With a 40 bps daily sd of the last-half-hour return and a 2.5 bps true mean, the expected t-stat is ≈1.1 — **under-powered**. Every daily-frequency hypothesis below therefore needs the 2016→2026 REST pull (≈2,500 sessions; expected t ≈3 if the pre-2013 effect were intact) with 2025–26 as the holdout.

## 4. What is testable with our data, in priority order

Data on hand: Alpaca SIP 1-min SPY 2025-01-02 → 2026-03-20 (local); Alpaca REST 1-min for any US symbol to 2016 (free); point-in-time daily bars for every US symbol 2025–2026 (delisted included). In this directory `fetch_etf_1min.py` is already populating `etf_1min.db` with SIP 1-min bars (o/h/l/c/v/n/vwap) for **SPY, QQQ, TQQQ, SQQQ, IWM, DIA, SOXL, UVXY from 2016-01-04** (≈8M bars loaded at the time of writing; resumable by symbol-month, `done` table records coverage), and `daily_panel.parquet` holds the daily panel for 15,448 symbols 2025-01-02 → 2026-09-04. That covers every "Yes" row below except H5's per-day top-20 stock 1-min pulls and H13's country ETFs.

1. **H3 (noise-area breakout, unlevered, VWAP+band stop)** — public code, one instrument, ≈450 trades/yr, three independent replications to compare against, and an author-reported 2024 OOS. This is the only rule here with a realistic net Sharpe prior ≥ 0.6. Run it with next-minute-open fills and 1 bp/trade before believing anything.
2. **H1/H2/H2b + H7 (last-30-min sign timing, unconditional and VIX-conditioned)** — trivially cheap on the same bars; the literature says unconditional is dead post-2013/2022 and conditional survives only in high vol. A clean negative on 2016–26 is worth having because our own HOD-break/ORB exits sit in that window.
3. **H4/H4b (5-min ORB on QQQ/TQQQ)** — REST pull of two tickers; expect rejection (Mesfin 2026, R4 base case) and treat a pass as a fills artefact until slippage is stressed.
4. **H9/H8/H10 conditioning** — only after H3 passes; they are IS findings in one paper.
5. **H5 (stocks-in-play ORB)** — do not build again; run as a parity check of R4's selection claim against our existing ORB feature pipeline (we already found the raw ORB edgeless and selection to be the edge).
6. **H11, H12, H13** — cheap add-ons or quick falsifications.
7. **H14, H15** — not testable / not worth testing.
