## 2. Every item, by theme

### Theme A — LLM/text signals for stock-return prediction and their pitfalls (11 items)
- Lopez-Lira & Tang (2023→2024, JFE) — arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619 — GPT-4 headline sentiment predicts next-day drift, ~90% hit rate on the non-tradable reaction — effect: drift shrinks as adoption rises (exact Sharpe path ~6.5→~1.2 circulating but unverified) — costs: not modelled by the paper — rebuttal: authors' own later versions self-document the decay — retail: not a standalone strategy at $65K today.
- Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev (2024, arXiv:2407.17866) — GPT-4 on anonymized financials beats analysts — effect: not reportable, superseded — costs: n/a — **rebuttal: SELF-WITHDRAWN 2025-02-20, co-author couldn't replicate** — retail: do not use.
- Glasserman & Lin (2023, arXiv:2309.17322) — anonymizing tickers before scoring outperforms in-sample, implying "distraction" not look-ahead dominates — effect: qualitative ranking only — costs: n/a — rebuttal: none, is itself the standard reference — retail: strip entity names before any LLM backtest scorer.
- Sarkar & Vafa (2024/ICML'25) — papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4754678 — general lookahead-bias placebo test, finds bias present in earnings-call and election tasks — effect: not extracted (paywalled) — costs: n/a — rebuttal: none, accepted at ICML 2025 — retail: validates OneMil's own decoy-placebo method, not tradable itself.
- Cao, Jiang, Wang & Yang (2024, JFE 160) — sciencedirect.com/S0304405X24001338 — AI analyst beats majority of human sell-side forecasters, humans retain edge on soft/institutional knowledge — effect: "beats the majority," exact numbers blocked — costs: n/a — rebuttal: none found — retail: needs proprietary I/B/E/S-style analyst panels, quarterly horizon — not feasible.
- Chen, Kelly & Xiu (2022–24) — papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416687 — LLM embeddings beat bag-of-words in a cross-sectional return model — effect: China replication ~53.4% directional accuracy, US headline number unverified — costs: n/a — rebuttal: partial cross-market replication with a smaller effect — retail: not operationally feasible, monthly panel + full-universe embeddings.
- Jha, Qian, Weber & Yang (2023/24, NBER WP 32161) — nber.org/papers/w32161 — "ChatGPT investment score" from earnings calls predicts capex up to 9 quarters ahead; high-score firms show negative future abnormal returns — effect: not extracted numerically — costs: n/a — rebuttal: none found — retail: quarterly-refresh signal, incompatible with intraday.
- Bybee, Kelly & Su (2023, RFS 36(12)) — narrative/topic-model factors beat standard factor models out-of-sample — effect: "higher OOS Sharpe, smaller pricing error," number unverified — costs: n/a — rebuttal: foundational pre-LLM baseline later papers build on — retail: monthly-rebalanced systematic tool, not an intraday signal.
- He, Lv, Manela & Wu (2025, arXiv:2502.21206, ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT) — trains LLMs on only point-in-time text to engineer out lookahead bias — effect: Sharpe "comparable to" a larger leakage-prone model — costs: n/a — rebuttal: itself a corrective response to Glasserman-Lin/Sarkar-Vafa — retail: infrastructure lesson — test for lookahead per application, never assume a generic pretrained model is safe.
- LoGrasso (2025, Modern Finance 3(3)) — mf-journal.com/article/view/327 — retrospective ChatGPT stock-picking, 1985–2022 — effect: raw CAR +27.24%/2yr window (p<0.001) but risk-adjusted alpha only 0.48–0.94%/mo, mostly insignificant, and the paper **assumes zero transaction costs** — rebuttal: its own honest caveats are the rebuttal — retail: caution, not a strategy; real costs would likely erase it.
- Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen (2020, JoF 75(3), pre-2023 but requested by brief) — "Lazy Prices": filing-language changes predict future earnings/bankruptcy — effect: up to 188 bps/month long-short — costs: no explicit modelling in the original, but an **open-source replication exists** (S&P 100, deflated Sharpe) — retail: buildable free on EDGAR text-diffs, but a slow multi-week filing-driven sleeve, not an intraday signal.

- Sheng, Sun, Yang & Zhang (2026, RFS/SSRN 4786575) — GenAI-adopting hedge funds earn +2–4%/yr abnormal return vs. non-adopters, driven by AI talent parsing firm-specific text — costs: fund NAV, implicitly net of fees — rebuttal: peer-reviewed at a top journal — retail: mechanism is analyst-augmentation by funds with in-house AI talent, not a buyable signal.
- Lopez-Lira & Tang (2023→JFE) — see Theme A/headline row 3 — repeated here as the practitioner-relevant version: edge decays with adoption, concentrated in small caps/negative news.

- Chen, Kelly & Malamud (2025, arXiv:2512.12735) — "Limits to (Machine) Learning": financial ML systematically UNDERSTATES true predictability due to a large finite-sample gap — effect: theoretical bound, "substantially large" — costs: n/a — retail: caution against over-reading a small in-house AUC/R² as proof of "no signal."
- Meng & Chen (2026, arXiv:2605.23905) — models AI-strategy alpha half-life compressing 5–7yr → ~18mo as adoption scales, portfolio convergence +42% (2013–24) — **peer-review status UNVERIFIED, authors/affiliation not independently confirmed** — retail: directionally consistent with the rest of the theme but do not repeat the specific numbers to the owner without a further check.

- AQR Capital Management (2023 practitioner paper) — explicit public position: "AI/ML cannot consistently predict future returns"; ML's clear wins are cost/risk/portfolio-construction plumbing — costs: n/a — retail: a top systematic manager with no commercial incentive to understate ML, saying it publicly, against retail "AI picker" marketing.

### Theme D — Reinforcement learning, LLM trading agents, deep learning on order books/intraday (14 items)
- Hambly, Xu & Yang (2023, Math Finance 33(3)) — survey of DRL in finance, flags most surveyed work lacks live validation/realistic costs — costs: n/a (survey) — retail: a field map, not an implementable edge.
- Wang & Saxena (2026, arXiv:2609.19705, "SoK") — adversarial audit of 15 published LLM-trading-agent schemes — effect: 80% fail a robustness metric, 100% exploitable across 3 attack categories — costs: n/a (robustness/security focus) — retail: do not deploy any multi-agent LLM framework unmodified on a live account.

- Xiao, Sun, Luo & Wang (2024/25, arXiv:2412.20138, "TradingAgents") — role-specialized multi-agent LLM framework claims to beat baselines — effect: "notable improvements" claimed, no numbers published — rebuttal: covered by the SoK audit and implicitly by FINSABER — retail: architecturally buildable on Alpaca+Benzinga, but unverified and shown fragile as a class.

- Lopez-Lira (2025, arXiv:2504.10789) — LLM agents in a simulated order-book market reproduce stylized market facts (bubbles, underreaction); correlated prompting can destabilize the simulated market — effect: qualitative, no P&L (simulation only) — retail: methodological — cheaply stress-test an agent design for herding before risking capital.

## 3. Cross-cutting lessons (merged across all four themes)
1. Look-ahead/leakage is the dominant recurring failure mode (Glasserman-Lin, Sarkar-Vafa, ChronoBERT's point-in-time fix) — the same failure class as OneMil's own PREREG_1478 store-identity leak; the field validates the causality-trace and shuffled-label-placebo discipline as necessary, not paranoid.
2. Even elite academic teams fail the independent-replication bar: Kim-Muhn-Nikolaev, the most-cited "GPT beats analysts" paper, was self-withdrawn in 2025 after a co-author couldn't replicate it. Treat any single-paper "AI beats X" headline as unverified until independently rebuilt — exactly OneMil's own rule.
3. Every measured AI/ML trading edge decays with adoption or scale (Lopez-Lira & Tang's own later versions, the NBER AI-hedge-fund 50bps→insignificant, Meng & Chen's modeled 5–7yr→18mo alpha half-life) — assume a short institutional shelf life, shorter still by the time retail can act on a published version.
4. Costs are the dominant unmodelled variable across the whole literature: Avramov-Cheng-Metzker and Jensen-Kelly-Malamud-Pedersen both show gross ML edges concentrate in illiquid/small/high-turnover names and shrink or invert net of realistic costs — mirrors OneMil's own "cost model is the null" finding; no paper in this sweep uses retail-realistic costs (10s latency, no depth-of-book, 25bps small-cap round-trip).
5. Where practitioners say ML actually pays off (Jane Street: sub-microsecond microstructure/execution; AQR: cost/risk/portfolio-construction plumbing) is structurally the domain a SIP-only, ~10s-latency $65K account cannot enter.
6. No retail-facing "AI stock picker" product (Danelfin, Kavout, Composer) has an independently audited, cost-inclusive live track record beating an index fund; the one product with a full honest live history (AIEQ) underperforms the S&P 500 net of its own fee.
7. The largest-sample, most cost-realistic industry number (Eurekahedge AI Hedge Fund Index, 14.5yr) says AI/ML hedge funds underperform passive equity net of real costs — the single most important expectation-setting fact across all four themes.
8. Every LLM multi-agent trading-agent paper (TradingAgents, FinMem, FinRobot) reports in-paper outperformance, and every independent re-test (SoK's adversarial audit, FINSABER's broad re-test, StockBench, LiveTradeBench) found the edge shrinks, disappears, or is exploitable — an in-sample-tuned-filter-evaporates-out-of-sample pattern OneMil already guards against in BF/ORB/HOD.
9. A model's general capability (chat leaderboard rank, static financial-QA score) does not predict its live trading skill (StockBench, LiveTradeBench, independently) — any LLM used for trading judgment must be evaluated on its own live/paper-trading run, never by proxy benchmark.
10. Real order-book-level predictability is genuine and reproducible (Kolm-Turiel-Westray, Lucchese et al.) but requires L2/L3 depth data the free Alpaca SIP feed does not provide, and neither paper tests economic significance net of costs — currently unimplementable on this account regardless of research merit.
11. Time-series foundation models (Chronos/TimesFM/Moirai) transplanted to financial returns are statistically indistinguishable from a random walk zero-shot — treat "foundation model" claims on finance with the same skepticism as any other data-mined signal.
12. The one credible mechanism for AI adding real, buyable value (Sheng/Sun/Yang/Zhang, RFS) is analyst-augmentation — funds with in-house AI talent reading firm-specific text faster and more thoroughly — not "the AI picks the trade." This is precisely the mechanism OneMil's own AI-native plan already leans on (reading and judgment speed, not signal generation).

## 4. What this changes for the plan (`research/passive_income/AI_NATIVE_PLAN_20260926.md`, legs A–E)
- **Leg A (overnight news sentiment):** confirmed real but small and decaying (Lopez-Lira & Tang); MUST use entity anonymization (Glasserman-Lin) and score only post-cutoff news (Sarkar-Vafa/ChronoBERT lesson) or the backtest will look better than the live signal ever will — the plan's own PREREG already calls for post-cutoff windows; this sweep confirms that's the right and necessary constraint, not over-caution.
- **Leg B (EDGAR/PR event desk):** the strongest supporting evidence in the whole sweep is Cohen-Malloy-Nguyen's "Lazy Prices" (188bps/mo, independently replicated, free EDGAR data) — a slow, multi-day filing-language-change sleeve, not intraday; the plan's medium-build timeline is right-sized for this evidence.
- **Leg C (news-spike fast leg):** no peer-reviewed evidence supports this leg at all; SoK/FINSABER/StockBench say fast autonomous LLM-agent decisions fail independent re-test 100% of the time in this literature — proceed only after the tape-measurement PREREG the plan already calls for, and never wire an agent framework to live orders unmodified.
- **Leg D (VRP income base):** untouched by this sweep (no options/vol-premium papers surfaced) — remains purely an owner decision, unaffected by the literature either way.
- **Leg E (intraday ML on own tape):** Avramov-Cheng-Metzker + Jensen-Kelly-Malamud-Pedersen both say ML edges live in illiquid names and vanish net of costs — consistent with cell 1,478–1,480's own decoy-AUC VOID result; the literature does not offer a rescue for this leg, only confirmation that costs must be charged before, not after, any signal is trusted.
- **Standing warning for every leg:** Kim-Muhn-Nikolaev's 2025 self-withdrawal is the sharpest evidence in the sweep that "no research claim ships without an independent check" is not bureaucratic overhead — it is the only thing that would have caught the field's single most-cited "AI beats analysts" claim before it became conventional wisdom.
